Reviewer 3

The paper provides a well-designed, technically solid framework for confidence-gated privileged-modality distillation using CT information to boost X-ray classification without the added deployment cost. The privileged mode, used only for training, is well motivated along with the proposed JDCNet architecture is described in sufficient depth for reproducibility. The extensive comparison to several knowledge distillation standards and the inclusion of deployment-cost assessment is greatly enhancing the contribution.

I would like to propose the following points for the further improvement of the text before approval:

While the study appropriately acknowledges the limitations of a single matched cohort, the discussion would be improved by a more clear plan of how the proposed framework would be validated in larger multi-institutional and multi-disease datasets, rather than focusing on binary COVID-19 classification only.
Although the confidence-gated procedure is clearly justified, additional qualitative visualizations (e.g., illustrative examples of accepted as well as rejected teacher predictions or attention maps) might improve readers' understanding of how the gating technique enhances the transfer of information.
The book offers full statistical assessments, but a brief summary of the practical therapeutic implications of the reported balanced accuracy gains would make it more accessible to a broader reading.
Details of computational complexity and effective implementation are well known. Adding a short discussion with recently published light-weight models for medical imaging would strengthen the discourse of practical application.
The external validation discourse, correctly, acknowledges the drop in performance on a different cohort. A short summary of potential domain adaptation or calibration methods can give further support to future research.

The study is nicely created and methodologically solid and makes a valuable contribution to the cross-modal medical image analysis discipline. The approach is robust, the experiments are comprehensive and the conclusions are sufficiently supported by the results given. I recommend a little revision.

Reviewer 4

In this paper, we propose a confidence-gated privileged-modality distillation architecture (JDCNet) to boost the chest X-ray classification by employing CT images only during training. The idea of keeping deployment expenses low while using privileged knowledge is important and the book contains detailed experimental evaluations. However, certain adjustments are needed to improve clarity, reproducibility and scientific importance.

We emphasize the novelty of JDCNet above the existing confidence-aware knowledge distillation and privileged information learning approaches. Despite the large amount of similar research, the specific methodological contribution has to be more explicitly highlighted.
Clarify the text. Many other sections (particularly Introduction, Related work, and Discussion) are repetitive and may be compressed without losing the scientific content.
Provide more details on implementation to improve reproducibility. More details are needed concerning the handling of random seeds, the preprocessing pipeline, the strategy of hyperparameter selection, and the use of computer resources.
The results of external validation show a substantial drop in model performance on an independent dataset. The limitation deserves additional investigation, such as checking domain shift, dataset properties, and various ways to boost generalization.
The statistical assessments are comprehensive. However, more statistical comparisons between the proposed technique along with the strongest competing baselines might strengthen the findings.
Add detailed discussion on potential clinical significance as well as practical implementation aspects including these regulatory challenges, integration into existing radiology workflows and actual world limitations.
Careful polishing of the language might improve the flow of sentences and remove unnecessary words, particularly in the techniques and discussion sections.
To improve understanding for a varied audience, consider adding a brief summary table outlining the primary benefits and downsides of JDCNet.

The proposed structure is technically attractive and supported by extensive testing. The above comments need to be addressed for the work to be clearer, more reproducible and of overall higher relevance. The manuscript is acceptable for publication following major revision.

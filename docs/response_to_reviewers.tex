The title is now ``JDCNet Uses Confidence Gated Privileged Modality Distillation
for Cost Preserving X Ray Inference''. It is used identically in the manuscript,
the separate title-page source, and the cover letter; punctuation and the former
hyphenated subtitle structure have been removed.

\paragraph{2. Select Yes for data availability and make the statement match the manuscript.}
The submission statement is now set to ``Yes. I used or generated research data
in this study.'' The manuscript and the editorial-system statement now point to
the BIMCV-COVID19+ portal at
\url{https://bimcv.cipf.es/bimcv-projects/bimcv-covid19} and explicitly identify
the BIMCV-COVID19 Research Use Agreement at
\url{https://bimcv.cipf.es/bimcv-projects/bimcv-covid19/bimcv-covid19-dataset-research-use-agreement-2/}.
The statement records that the data are de-identified, that re-identification
and data sharing are prohibited, and that no image pixels or individual image
files are redistributed. Only derived aggregate results and anonymized
case-level gate summaries are reported; split definitions, aggregate results,
and analysis manifests are available at
\url{https://github.com/mabo1215/JDCNET.git}.

\paragraph{3. Add a Declarations section with separate ethical approval, consent to participate, and consent to publish headings.}
Done. The manuscript now contains a separate \textit{Declarations} section with
individual \textit{Ethical approval}, \textit{Consent to participate}, and

recruitment or data collection. That agreement permits research use but
prohibits sharing, re-identification, and publication or reproduction of any
dataset portion without specific prior written permission. We therefore provide
the official BIMCV data-portal URL, not patient image files or pixels.

\section*{Reviewer 3}

\paragraph{1. Clarify how the framework will be validated on larger multi-institutional and multi-disease datasets.}
We agree. The Limitations and Future Work section now specifies a validation
programme rather than only a general aspiration. The next study will assemble
same-patient CT--X-ray cohorts from multiple hospitals, use site-held-out
evaluation, and harmonize label definitions before extending the task from
COVID-19 versus non-COVID to multi-disease classification (for example,
pneumonia, tuberculosis, edema, and no-finding categories). The transfer gate,
paired bootstrap, and per-fold win-count reporting will be fixed before those
data are evaluated. These are explicitly described as planned validation steps,
not as results established by the present single-cohort study.

\paragraph{2. Add qualitative visualizations of accepted and rejected teacher predictions or attention maps.}
We added a new qualitative gate-behaviour figure, an aggregate qualitative
visualization of the gate behavior. It contrasts the retained subset
($\mathrm{ECE}=0.07$--$0.08$ and retained-minus-rejected teacher accuracy of
$+0.11$--$+0.14$) with the rejected subset ($\mathrm{ECE}=0.14$--$0.16$), and
shows the DRR failure mode in which confidence does not identify a reliable
subset. The underlying per-teacher coverage and reliability values remain in
the gate-coverage table. JDCNet uses a ResNet-18 backbone and contains
no attention module; attention maps would therefore add an unvalidated
interpretation rather than visualize a mechanism used by the method. We label
the new panel as an aggregate diagnostic, not as an individual-patient
explanation.
To answer the request for real-sample context while respecting
the BIMCV Research Use Agreement, we added a four-row case-level numerical
table to the revised manuscript, generated from the retained mid-slice
teacher checkpoint, the fold-0/seed-42 manifest, and the available de-identified
CT cache. It reports teacher confidence, teacher prediction, ground truth, and
gate outcome at $\tau_{\mathrm{gate}}=0.80$. The examples include accepted and
rejected predictions, including an accepted incorrect prediction and a rejected
incorrect prediction, so that acceptance is visibly distinct from correctness.
The table uses newly assigned case labels, cites the official BIMCV data portal
as the source collection, and does not reproduce pixels, individual image URLs,
filenames, or patient identifiers. It is explicitly labelled as illustrative
context rather than a new performance estimate.

\paragraph{3. Explain the practical or therapeutic implications of the balanced-accuracy gains.}
We added a practical interpretation immediately after the absolute-performance
table. For the strongest 3-slice soft-KL cell, balanced accuracy increases from
$0.604$ to $0.639$, sensitivity from $0.547$ to $0.598$, and specificity from
$0.661$ to $0.679$. This indicates why the result could matter for X-ray triage
or prioritisation: more positive cases are detected at the reported balanced
operating point without a specificity decrease in this cohort. We also state
explicitly that these are retrospective model metrics, not evidence of improved
treatment decisions, patient outcomes, or therapeutic benefit; those claims
require prospective workflow and outcome studies.

\paragraph{4. Discuss recently published lightweight medical-imaging models.}
The Related Work section now positions MobileNetV2 and EfficientNet as relevant
lightweight deployment backbones. The Deployment-Time Efficiency section also
reports a portability check using MobileNetV2 and EfficientNet-B0 students with
the same gated supervision: the observed gains are positive but slightly below
the pre-specified gate ($+0.026$ and $+0.029$, respectively). This strengthens
the practical discussion while keeping the claim bounded: the validated
headline deployment path remains the ResNet-18 student, and the lightweight
results are portability evidence rather than independent clinical validation.

\paragraph{5. Expand the external-validation discussion with domain adaptation and calibration options.}
The Limitations section now separates acquisition-protocol shift,
label-definition/prevalence shift, and population/scanner shift as plausible
causes of the external collapse. It discusses multi-center training,
unsupervised or lightly supervised feature/input domain adaptation, and
post-hoc site-specific threshold recalibration as concrete next experiments.
The manuscript also reports the teacher-temperature ablation in the Appendix.
We make clear that none of the proposed
mitigations has been validated in this paper and that the external X-ray stress
test does not replace independent same-patient paired replication.

\section*{Reviewer 4}

\paragraph{1. Make the novelty relative to confidence-aware KD and privileged-information methods explicit.}
We added the Methodology subsection ``Design Rationale versus Confidence-Aware
and Confidence-Based Distillation'' and the Related Work comparison in
the SOTA comparison table. The revised text distinguishes JDCNet along two
axes: a hard per-sample confidence mask rather than continuous reliability
weighting, and a discrete or lightly softened auxiliary target rather than a
fully softened cross-modal logit distribution. It also compares modality
pairing, same-patient evaluation, fixed-cost deployment auditing, target
granularity, and failed-channel reporting against recent privileged-transfer
and confidence-aware approaches. The claim is stated as a joint methodological
and evaluation contribution, not as a claim that confidence gating itself is
new in every domain.

\paragraph{2. Compress repetitive material in the Introduction, Related Work, and Discussion.}
We shortened the repeated positioning paragraph in the Introduction and
condensed the Discussion's broad clinical-imaging implications so that they
refer back to the design-rationale subsection rather than restating the full
mechanistic argument. Related Work now uses the comparison table to carry the
repeated method-by-method distinctions, while retaining only the differences
needed to establish the scope and novelty of JDCNet. The revision preserves
the evidence limitations and citations while removing duplicated cost and
cross-modal-transfer explanations.

\paragraph{3. Add implementation details for seeds, preprocessing, hyperparameter selection, and computer resources.}
The Implementation Details section specifies ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18
initialization, seeds 42--44, patient-level five-fold cross-validation, the
$224\times224$ resize, augmentation, channel replication for CT slices,
weighted sampling, weighted cross-entropy, AdamW settings, 50 epochs, the
pre-specified $\tau$ and $\lambda$ grid, and the RTX 3090 environment. It also
reports the approximately six-hour two-GPU sweep, the approximately $1.18\times$
training-time overhead, and the measured deployment cost on CPU, GPU, and edge
hardware. The Appendix gives the cohort construction and leakage audit.

\paragraph{4. Investigate the performance drop on the independent dataset.}
We expanded the External-Cohort Stress Test and Limitations sections. The
frozen supervised and JDCNet students all fall to chance on the external
X-ray-only cohort, so the result is reported as a source-to-target
generalization warning rather than a JDCNet-specific failure. We now discuss
acquisition, population, scanner, label, and prevalence shift and identify
multi-center training, domain adaptation, and site-specific calibration as
follow-up approaches. The manuscript does not present any of these approaches
as already validated.

\paragraph{5. Add stronger statistical comparisons.}
The main decision-summary table now reports the paired $\Delta\mathrm{BA}$,
bootstrap confidence interval, Wald-type $z$, two-sided $p$, and paired Cohen's
$d$ for the headline rows and strongest competing baselines. The primary
decision remains the pre-specified CI-based transfer gate with
Benjamini--Hochberg correction; the named tests and effect sizes are reported
as complementary diagnostics. Both passing JDCNet settings remain significant
($p=0.003$ and $p=0.011$), while the strongest failed gated-logit comparator is
not significant ($p=0.19$).

\paragraph{6. Expand clinical significance, workflow integration, regulatory issues, and real-world limitations.}
The revised manuscript interprets the absolute sensitivity and specificity
changes, explains that CT is removed before deployment, and discusses how an
X-ray-only input path could fit a radiology workflow without requiring CT at
inference. It also lists the unmet requirements before clinical use:
multi-site external validation, prospective outcome-based evaluation,
site-specific threshold calibration, and regulatory assessment of the deployed
X-ray decision-support software. The benefits-and-limitations table summarizes
these benefits, limitations, and implementation risks in one place.

\paragraph{7. Polish the language and summarize the main advantages and disadvantages.}
We performed a language pass over the Introduction, Methods, Results, and
Discussion, removing repeated claims and replacing broad deployment language
with evidence-bounded wording. The benefits-and-limitations table now provides
the requested compact summary: zero inference-time CT cost and the observed
balanced-accuracy gain are listed alongside training overhead, dependence on a
paired single-cohort dataset, lack of prospective clinical evidence, and
outstanding regulatory and calibration requirements.

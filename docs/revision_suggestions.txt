Reviewer 1

The manuscript titled “JDCNet Uses Confidence Gated Privileged Modality Distillation for Cost Preserving X Ray Inference” requires substantial improvement in terms of methodological clarity, comparative depth, and language precision. The following comments aim to guide the authors toward significant enhancement:
Why is the evaluation only done on COVID-19 diagnosis and can this approach be extended to multi-class (multi-disease) problems?
How do you justify the performance drop of the model (BA approx 0.49) on the external data (MIDRC) and what is your solution to deal with domain changes?
Given the drastic changes in performance by moving the ζgate and λ parameters, how likely is overfitting on this dataset?
The manuscript relies heavily on sources before 2021. Consider this works:
https://doi.org/10.1007/s11227-024-06385-2, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-28737-5 , https://doi.org/10.1007/s11227-025-07983-4 , https://doi.org/10.1007/s11227-024-06806-2 , 10.22067/jam.2023.81735.1157 , https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-27314-7_22
How much valuable uncertainty information is lost by replacing the teacher's soft distribution with hard argmax values?
Why weren't corrective techniques such as Temperature Scaling used to address possible overconfidence in the teacher model before applying the confidence gate?

Reviewer 6

This manuscript evaluates JDCNet, a confidence-gated CT-to-X-ray distillation framework in which CT is used only during training and the deployed model receives chest X-rays alone. The revised manuscript makes a commendable effort to report negative comparator results, external-cohort failure, deployment cost, and limitations rather than presenting only favorable experiments.

Comments:

(1) Clarify whether CT teachers were trained separately within each cross-validation fold
The manuscript states that each CT teacher view was “pre-trained once” and that the frozen checkpoint was reused across student runs. It is unclear whether this means one teacher was trained within each training fold and reused only across seeds and student configurations belonging to that fold, or one teacher was trained on the complete 510-patient cohort and then used in all five cross-validation folds. The second procedure would expose the teacher to labels or images from patients later used as held-out test cases.

(2) The manuscript evaluates 16 JDCNet configurations on the same 510-patient cohort and designates the two configurations that satisfy the decision gate as validated operating points. The same cross-validation predictions are therefore used both to select the configurations and to estimate their performance. The Benjamini-Hochberg procedure may address one aspect of multiple hypothesis testing, but it does not remove the optimism introduced by selecting configurations and reporting their cross-validated performance on the same patients. Moreover, the manuscript does not provide the directly calculated \(p\)-values used for the correction.

(3) The external stress test is valuable and should remain prominent. However, the manuscript states that the three methods are “statistically indistinguishable” without reporting confidence intervals or a statistical comparison. Please report patient-level confidence intervals for BA and AUC and paired differences between each JDCNet model and the supervised baseline. This can be calculated from the existing predictions.

The assertion that the failure reflects source shift “rather than a JDCNet-specific weakness” is plausible but not demonstrated. The data show that all tested models fail; they do not identify the cause of failure. Acquisition protocol, label definition, prevalence, scanner, population, and preprocessing differences should be presented as possible explanations.
A directly relevant reference the authors may consider in this discussion is
[1] Zhang J, Chao H, Dhurandhar A, Chen P-Y, Tajer A, Xu Y, Yan P. When Neural Networks Fail to Generalize? A Model Sensitivity Perspective. Proceedings of the AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence. 2023;37(9):11219–11227.
This work is relevant to the manuscript’s single-source generalization failure and the need to distinguish internal cross-validation from robustness to unseen distributions.

(4) The manuscript is generally cautious, but the following statements should be further moderated.
- “X-ray triage or prioritisation” is not evaluated as a clinical workflow.
- Matching parameter count, MACs, and single-image latency does not establish equal end-to-end deployment cost, calibration, safety, or clinical utility.
- A higher retrospective sensitivity does not by itself demonstrate better triage.
- Describing the software as following the “same premarket evaluation pathway” involves regulatory assumptions outside the presented experiment.
Please restrict the conclusion to the demonstrated result: the deployed computation graph is identical to the supervised ResNet-18 baseline and therefore has no incremental model-inference cost under the reported benchmark.

(5) Others:
- The “CT teacher upper bound” is not a mathematical upper bound on student performance. “Privileged-modality reference” or “CT-only reference” would be more accurate.

- The statement that the two passing configurations recover “roughly two-thirds of the available gap” is a ratio of uncertain and selected estimates. Either provide uncertainty for this ratio or describe it explicitly as a descriptive calculation.

- Clarify whether the reported F1 is binary F1 or macro-F1. Table 5 labels it only as “F1.”

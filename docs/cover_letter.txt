WHAT THE PAPER STUDIES

We investigate whether computed tomography (CT), available only during model
development, can improve an X-ray-only classifier without changing the
deployed model. JDCNet applies an auxiliary CT-teacher target only on training
samples that the teacher classifies confidently. At test time the deployed
model is a standard ResNet-18 X-ray classifier with the same computation graph
as the supervised baseline.

ACCURACY OF REPORTING AND NEW EVIDENCE

Following the Editor's request that results be accurately reported, we
retrieved the complete per-run records of the original experiments and
re-audited every number in the manuscript. The audit led to substantive
corrections, which are listed openly at the start of the response letter.
Most importantly, the 16 JDCNet configurations were explored adaptively rather
than registered in advance, and the supervised baseline had been trained with
a larger batch size than JDCNet. Several previously reported quantities could
not be traced to any record and have been removed or recomputed.

To address these issues and Reviewer 6's concerns about selection optimism,
we wrote an analysis plan and committed it before running a supplementary
experiment on the original splits. The experiment comprised 240 batch-matched
supervised restarts, per-patient predictions for every checkpoint, and
teacher-calibration diagnostics.

MAIN FINDINGS (REVISED)

On 510 same-patient paired patients from the public BIMCV-COVID19 releases:

  - 15 of 16 JDCNet configurations improved mean balanced accuracy (BA) over
    the supervised baseline (average +0.020), but no individual configuration
    is significant after Holm adjustment.
  - The training budget is unlikely to explain the results: supervised BA was
    0.620 at JDCNet's batch size versus 0.625 originally.
  - In the pre-registered nested comparison, both methods selected one of 16
    runs per cross-validation cell on the validation partition. JDCNet
    exceeded supervised training by +0.037 BA (95% CI +0.016 to +0.058;
    patient-level +0.007 to +0.068).
  - Logit distillation and contrastive alignment showed no gain.
  - Teacher diagnostics indicate that the gate acts mainly as CT-informed
    sample weighting.
  - All models, including JDCNet, performed at chance on an external MIDRC
    radiograph set.

The conclusion is therefore a small, method-level advantage within one public
cohort and a binary task, with no evidence of external generalization.

RELEVANCE TO DISCOVER ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

Training-only privileged modalities arise wherever a richer modality is
available during development but not at the point of care. Examples include
CT for X-ray, whole-slide imaging for cytology, and OCT for fundus
photography. Beyond the method itself, the paper offers a worked example of
selection-aware evaluation on a small paired medical cohort: full reporting of
the search, multiplicity adjustment, and a pre-registered nested comparison
with a matched control.

WHAT THE PAPER STUDIES

We investigate whether computed tomography (CT), available only during model
development, can improve an X-ray-only classifier without changing the
deployed model. JDCNet applies an auxiliary CT-teacher target only on training
samples that the teacher classifies confidently. At test time the deployed
model is a standard ResNet-18 X-ray classifier with the same computation graph
as the supervised baseline.

ACCURACY OF REPORTING

Following the Editor's request that results be accurately reported, we
re-audited every number in the manuscript against our archived experiment
records and analysis scripts. The audit led to substantive corrections, which
are listed openly at the start of the response letter. Most importantly, the
16 JDCNet configurations were explored adaptively on the same 510-patient
cohort rather than registered in advance, and the supervised baseline was
trained with a larger batch size than JDCNet. In addition, several previously
reported quantities could not be traced to a record and have been removed.
These include an absolute-metrics table, a gate-coverage table, lightweight-
backbone results, patient-level bootstrap intervals, and latency figures. The
statistics in the revised manuscript are regenerated by released scripts from
released aggregate records.

MAIN FINDINGS (REVISED)

On 510 same-patient paired patients from the public BIMCV-COVID19 releases,
under patient-level five-fold cross-validation with three seeds:

  - 15 of 16 JDCNet configurations improved mean balanced accuracy (BA) over
    the supervised baseline; the average improvement was +0.020.
  - The two best configurations improved BA by +0.035 (95% CI +0.011 to
    +0.057) and +0.033 (95% CI +0.007 to +0.058). They were selected from 16
    configurations, and their Holm-adjusted p-values are 0.051 and 0.17. The
    eight initially planned configurations average +0.016 (95% CI -0.000 to
    +0.035).
  - Logit distillation and contrastive alignment showed no gain on the same
    splits.
  - Batch-matched reruns were mixed.
  - All students, supervised and JDCNet alike, performed at chance on an
    external radiograph set.

We therefore present confidence-gated CT supervision as a plausible but
unconfirmed way to use training-only CT, and we specify the confirmatory
study that is needed.

RELEVANCE TO DISCOVER ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

Training-only privileged modalities arise wherever a richer modality is
available during development but not at the point of care. Examples include
CT for X-ray, whole-slide imaging for cytology, and OCT for fundus
photography. Beyond the method itself, the paper offers a worked example of
transparent, selection-aware evaluation on a small paired medical cohort,
and shows how such reporting changes the conclusion.
